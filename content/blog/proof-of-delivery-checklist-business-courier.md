A delivery isn't finished for your office until someone can find the record later. This checklist helps Las Vegas valley teams save, label and file courier delivery confirmations so anyone can quickly answer three questions: when did it arrive, who received it, and which job was it for?

## What Proof of Delivery Means for Your Office

For office recordkeeping, proof of delivery is the confirmation your team saves after a handoff. Its contents vary by carrier and service. USPS, for example, describes its [return receipt as proof of delivery](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Return-Receipt-The-Basics) that can include the recipient's signature and delivery details.

Falcon Urgent Delivery describes its proof of delivery as a photo, the delivery time and the recipient's name. Fields can change, so check current details before you book.

## Save the Confirmation the Same Day

When the delivery email, photo or receipt arrives, move it into your team's designated system that day rather than relying solely on an inbox or tracking page. This matters most when the delivery relates to an open repair order, client file, purchase order or vendor return.

At minimum, save the internal reference, delivery date and time, recipient or confirmation type, and where the record is stored.

Retention is a policy decision. NIST's [electronic file organization tips](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2022/03/30/ElectronicFileOrganizationTips-2016-03.pdf) say records should be maintained according to the organization's own retention schedule. Follow yours, and ask your advisers about any legal or regulatory requirements.

## Proof of Delivery Checklist for Business Courier Records

Capture these details for every completed run:

- Internal reference number (work order, matter, PO or ticket)
- Delivery date and time
- Recipient name or receiving desk, if your team needs it
- Pickup business and receiving business
- Delivery status and confirmation type
- Where the confirmation photo, email or attachment is saved
- Staff member who requested the run

Keep it lean. Record only the personal details your team needs, store them in your approved internal system, and leave them out of filenames and shared logs.

## Name Files So Coworkers Can Find Them

NIST notes that a filename should be clear to the people who will use it, and that including a relevant date helps prevent confusion when files are copied or moved. Federal records guidance from the National Archives offers a similar convention: [consistent, meaningful file and folder names](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/bulletins/2015/2015-04-appendix-b.html), with elements such as dates kept in the same position. The habit works for private offices too.

A simple pattern:

`YYYY-MM-DD_internal-reference_delivery-confirmation.pdf`

Adapt it to your existing system, and avoid vague labels such as "final," "new" or "delivery stuff."

## Link the Record to the Related Work

Attach or link the confirmation to the repair order, client matter, vendor return or office request it belongs to. A shared reference number helps prevent mix-ups when several deliveries go to the same building on one day. If more than one department is involved, agree on who owns the record.

Organize folders by year, month, department or job type, whichever matches how your team already works.

## Review Exceptions Before Closing the Request

Before marking a run complete, check for:

- A recipient name that doesn't match who you expected
- A missing, unclear or unopenable attachment
- An unexpected delivery status
- No internal reference attached

If something looks incomplete or inconsistent, [contact the courier](/contact) promptly while details are fresh.
